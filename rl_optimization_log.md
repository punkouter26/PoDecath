Trainer: MuJoCo Warp + custom PPO (`training/ppo.py`), 4096 GPU environments, RTX 5070 Ti Laptop.

## Phase 1 — metric discovery and baseline

### 1.1 Audit

**Model** (`training/models/athlete.xml`, generated by `rig_to_mjcf.py` from the Mixamo rig)

| Item | Value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Timestep / integrator | 0.005 s, `implicitfast`, 10 solver iterations, 10 line-search iterations | sound; control at 50 Hz (decimation 4) |
| Joints | 22 hinge (21 actuated + root), all with anatomical `range`, `damping 0.5`, `armature 0.02-0.1` | limits present on every joint |
| Actuators | `<position>` with per-joint `kp`/`kv`, `forcerange` (e.g. abdomen ±170 N m), `ctrlrange` = joint range | force-limited from human data (house rule 15) |
| Contacts | every geom `contype=1 conaffinity=1`; 11 `<contact><exclude>` parent-child pairs | already what the goal asks for |
| Friction | 1.0 / 0.005 / 0.0001 on feet and floor | standard |

**Environment** (`training/envs/run_to_target.py`, `run_track.py`)

- Observation: 80 floats = base state (12) + 3 × 21 joint channels + command (3) + gait clock (2). Normalised by a
  running mean/variance in PPO (`obs_rms`, clipped at ±10).
- Reward: 21 terms. Tracking kernel `1.5·exp(-(v-v*)²/track_var)` (track_var 2.0), progress `0.25·v`, alive/upright/
  heading (posture), height, vertical velocity, angular velocity, **action cost** `-0.002·|a|²`, **action rate**
  `-0.02·|Δa|²`, joint-limit proximity, air time per footfall, foot slip, swing clearance, stance width, double-support,
  arm swing, **energy** `-2e-4·|τ·q̇|`, **joint speed over human limit**, gait-clock contact schedule, fall `-2`.
  The goal's "ctrl cost" and "acceleration spike" penalties therefore exist already (r_act, r_rate, r_energy, r_qvel).
- Termination: pelvis below 60 % stand height, upright < 0.4, non-finite, off the 5.3 m deck (track), 20 s timeout.

**Metrics already instrumented** (`_accumulate_kpis`, written per iteration to CSV and TensorBoard `env/*`):
`surv_ratio`, `fall_rate`, `v_err`, `v_hit_frac`, `torque`, `power`, `jerk`, `pitch_dev`, `roll_dev`, `act_sat`,
`duty_factor`, `air_time`, `foot_slip`, `tgt_clamp`, plus every reward term as `rt_*`.
So the four KPI families the goal names (survival, velocity error, control cost / jerk, torso pitch/roll) were
already logged by a previous session. What was **missing** is an *evaluation* that scores consecutive deterministic
episodes against thresholds; added in 1.2.

**PPO** (`ppo.py`): actor/critic MLP 512-256-128, 24 steps × N envs per iteration, 5 epochs, 4 minibatches
(24,576 samples each at 4096 envs), γ 0.99, λ 0.95, clip 0.2, value coef 1.0, grad-norm 1.0, entropy 0.005,
KL-adaptive LR (target 0.01, ×/÷ 1.5 per minibatch, floor 1e-5, ceiling 1e-2), obs clip 10. CUDA graphs on.
Vectorisation is already GPU-stepped (MuJoCo Warp), no per-step Python allocation on the hot path.

### 1.2 Instrumentation added

- `training/eval_lap.py`: `--episodes N` runs N consecutive deterministic episodes (full reset between them),

**Lessons from batch 1.** (a) A control run that changes nothing still moves the gate score by
-0.08 m/s, so single-experiment deltas under ~3 % are noise, and the plateau rule (<3 % over three
runs) is calibrated to that. (b) Sharpening the tracking kernel does nothing because the policy is
nowhere near the kernel's interesting region — at 3.3 vs a 4.0 command the Gaussian's gradient is
still gentle at var 2.0. What moved the needle was paying more for *going forward* (progress weight),
which says the binding constraint is the incentive balance against posture income (0.79/step), not
the shape of the tracking curve. (c) More exploration (x3) on a policy that already knows the gait
destroys it — exploration is for the get-up task, not here.

**Exit-gate accounting note.** The gate starts every athlete from a standstill and divides total
travel by the full 20 s window, so the acceleration phase is inside every speed figure, training
`v_err` included. That is deliberate (a race includes the start) and stays the official number; but
it means "cruise speed" and "window speed" are different quantities, and batch 2 adds a warm-start
diagnostic to tell them apart before more incentive tuning is trusted.

### 2.3 The warm-start diagnostic changes the diagnosis (01:16)

`eval_lap.py --init-speed 3.0` (episodes begin at U[0, 3] m/s) on x6's checkpoint:

| start | speed | clean |
|---|---|---|
| standing (official gate) | 3.34 | 100 % |
| **already running at up to 3 m/s** | **3.30** | **46 %** |

Given momentum, the athletes *fall over half the time*, and the survivors are no faster. So the
policy does not cruise at ~3.4 and fall short of 4.0 — it never reaches a cruise at all. The whole
20 s window is one continuous, stability-limited acceleration, and the gate's 3.60 requirement is
blocked by a **stability-at-speed ceiling**, not by weak speed incentives. That is consistent with
every batch-1/2 null result: sharper tracking (x1), more progress weight (x7), more gradient work
(x5) and at-speed resets (x6) all push *toward* the same wall.

What sets a stability ceiling at speed? Prime suspects, all trainable in short runs:
- **The gait schedule is a walk.** `gait-duty 0.6` asks each foot for 60 % stance and leaves 20 % of
  the stride in double support. At 4 m/s a human is running (duty < 0.5). Measured duty is already
  0.45-0.47 — the athletes are running *against* the clock they are being paid to match.
- **Stride period 0.8 s** at 4 m/s is a 3.2 m stride; cadence may need to be higher for this rig.
- **No tilt-fade on speed income** (`vel-gate 0`): topple-toward-target is paid like running-toward
  target exactly in the regime where toppling is what kills.

All four resume from `lap_v80` with `--prog-w 0.5` (batch 1's one keeper) plus one stability lever;

- **x9_period06** `--gait-period 0.6` — higher cadence, shorter strides.

**(01:55) x9_period06: cold gate 2.93 m/s** — the 0.6 s stride period made the deterministic gait

### 2.8 What this means (recommendations for the owner)

1. **The 3.4 m/s wall is real and specific.** It is not weak incentives, wrong PPO knobs, or the gait
   schedule — each family got its experiment. It is the top speed at which the *v80 rig's* current
   control policy family can stay upright, and every policy fine-tuned from `lap_v80` inherits it.
   Passing the 3.60 gate needs a policy that learns to run fast *from scratch* with a speed
   curriculum (target 2.0 → 4.5 m/s over thousands of iterations, as `--speed-adaptive` anticipates)
   rather than a fine-tune of a policy trained at lower speeds. That is a fresh multi-hour run, not a
   tweak; it did not fit this session once the ceiling was diagnosed.
2. **Ship decision is the owner's.** x2 (`checkpoints/run_track/x2_progw05/latest.pt`) is strictly
   better than what lap_v80 offers — +0.10 m/s at identical 100 % clean rate and near-identical
   control cost over 30 episodes. If 3.44 vs 3.34 m/s matters for the game, export it with
   `PoDecath/Refresh Policy Library` after copying the ONNX from
   `training/logs/scratch_policies/` — nothing was published autonomously; shipping is a decision.
3. **Instrumentation added this session stays useful**: `eval_lap.py --init-speed` (warm-start
   diagnostic — measures the stability-at-speed ceiling directly; recommend adding it to any future
   lap-policy acceptance check), per-experiment `.args.json` provenance, and the batch runner
   pattern (`x_batch*.ps1`) which survives what the bash runner did not.
4. **The get-up side of the house finished strong**: `getup_v80b` completed 3000/3000 iterations and
   shipped `athlete_getup.onnx`. Recommended follow-up: `PoDecath/Probe Get-Up Transfer` on the new
   export, comparing `peak_upright` / `longest_hold_s` against the previous 0.947 / 7.44 s.

### 3.4 Root cause chain closed with MuJoCo-side controls (11:30)

Rolling the same checkpoint in the MuJoCo get-up env from forced flat-supine resets
(`training/check_getup_mj.py`), with and without Unity's action clip:

| rollout | actions | result over 6 s |
|---|---|---|
| MuJoCo, unclamped | mean \|a\| 8.6, peaks ±23 | **stands and holds**: upright −0.26 → **0.991** (2 s) → 0.958 (4 s) → 0.995 (6 s) |
| MuJoCo, clamped ±3 (Unity-faithful) | mean \|a\| 2.8, max 3.0 | **rises, then collapses**: −0.32 → 0.991 (2 s) → −0.16 (4 s) → −0.19 (6 s) |
| Unity/PhysX, clamp ±3 | pinned at ±3 by config | never rises (peak = start value) |
| Unity/PhysX, clip raised to 30 mid-run | (change applied near run start; result ambiguous — still flat at final) | never rises |

Read together with the training code: the get-up env (`run_to_target.py:53`) clamps actions to ±3
during training, and with `action_scale` 0.167 that caps every joint target at default ±0.5 rad —
the policy is trained into a torque envelope that can rise from supine but **cannot hold the
stand** (even in MuJoCo it collapses by 4 s; the env's own `hold` metric was only 0.46). The training
rollout clamp was verified absent from `ppo.py` and present in `run_to_target.py:460`; the Unity
`actionClip=3` in the per-athlete config (`Matt RL_config`, written from the manifest's
`action_clip: 3.0` in `Assets/Models/athlete_policy_config.json`, authored by `rig_to_mjcf.py`) is
therefore *faithful* to training — **the training env's own clamp is the limitation**, and PhysX adds
a further rise failure on top.

Secondary bug found and fixed on the way: `PolicyRunner.Step()` ticked a *disabled*
`VelocityCommandSource` (it only checked `commandSource != null`), so the probe's lying athlete was
being steered by a stale saturated command (measured `(1.9, −3, 3)`); the fix adds an
`isActiveAndEnabled` check. Real bug, but not the stand-breaker.

**Why the 0.947 benchmark worked on 09-13:** that was the **09-05-era 75-observation policy**
(`9d3d422`, 825,583 bytes) on pre-gait-migration code — a different policy and contract from
everything tested above. The control model I first extracted from `4e71663` turned out to be an
early iter-~50 export of the *new* 80-obs lineage (835,863 bytes), which is why it failed too.

### 3.5 Paths forward (owner decision)

- **A. Retrain get-up with a sane action envelope** (raise the env clamp from ±3 to ≥ ±10, or drop
  it and rely on actuator ctrlrange): direct fix for the marginal-hold problem; ~2-4 h of training.
  The 09-13 evidence (0.947 in PhysX) says transfer itself is achievable once the policy is not
  envelope-starved.
- **B. Adopt the DeepMind MuJoCo Unity plugin** (google-deepmind/mujoco/tree/main/unity — official,
  actively maintained, MuJoCo 3.13.1, imports `athlete.xml` directly): the game would step the
  athlete with the *same engine it trains in*, eliminating this transfer-bug class by construction.
  Cost: every athlete-runtime system is PhysX-coupled (`PolicyRunner` drives, `RecoveryController`,
  `FootContactSensor` collision callbacks, `EffortMeter` driveForce, fall detection, skinning
  binding) and would need porting to MjBody/MjActuator/touch-sensor components; environment colliders
  need MuJoCo geoms; mobile (Android) performance is unproven; Unity 6 compatibility needs a spike.
  Docs recommend stable tags, not `main`.
- Recommendation: **A now** (hours, unblocks the demo), **B as a time-boxed spike later** (import
  `athlete.xml` in a scratch scene, stand the athlete, benchmark Android) if transfer bugs keep
  recurring after A.
