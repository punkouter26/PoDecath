Overnight speed training

29–30 September 2026, 23:14 → 07:18 (8 h 4 min) · MuJoCo Warp, 4,096 athletes at once, RTX 2060 · batch Q (training/run_q.ps1 + run_q_extend.ps1)

In one breath: the goal was to teach the athlete to run faster than the 3.4 m/s wall. It didn't get there. The best result is 2.76 m/s round the rooftop lap with zero falls in 10 test runs. That beats the lap runner the game ships today (about 2.2 m/s), but not the 3.34 m/s best from 15 September, whose saved files are no longer on this PC. The night's main lesson is a fault in the "speed up only when safe" rule. That rule wasted about 3 of the 8 hours, and the fix is small.

The three charts that matter

Chart 1 · the pace we asked for (env/target_speed)

Toddler

We said "run faster!" too soon. The runner wobbled, so we said "slow down", and we never said "faster" again.

Child

The coach raised the goal from 1 to 5.5 m/s in about 20 minutes. The runner only ever reached 1.65. When the practice got harder later on, the coach dropped the goal right back to walking pace, and it stayed there for three hours.

Adult

The adaptive curriculum raises the target 0.05 m/s every time falls are under 5 % and lowers it when they pass 15 %. Two flaws show up here. First, it climbs on falls alone and never checks whether the athlete keeps up, so the target outran the body by about 4 m/s. Second, the random shoves and friction changes ramp up to full strength over the first 2,000 rounds of each stage, and at full strength the walk falls 15–24 % of the time. So the target backed off to its 1.0 floor, and the "under 5 %" condition was never met again. The same thing happened to the orange lap stage at 2.5 m/s. The purple extra stage restarted the ramp at gentle shoves, which is why it climbed again.

Chart 2 · how fast it actually went (env/v_toward)

Toddler

It learned to walk really fast. Then it jogged a bit, then went back to walking. On the roof it ran!

Child

Walking came in about 10 minutes, at just under 1 m/s. Open-ground running peaked at 1.65 m/s and faded once the goal was cut. The rooftop stage jumped straight to 2.5 m/s, because following a moving marker round a track pulls it along. In the final 11 minutes it was still speeding up.

Adult

The walk came from scratch in about 350 rounds, far faster than the 15 September lineage managed. That recipe (cheap falls, then firm) is sound and reusable. The speed lost in stage 3 matches the target cut in chart 1 exactly: it's the curriculum, not the body's limit. The purple segment rose 2.47 → 2.67 m/s in 300 rounds with the target held at 5.0, so the body has more speed in it. The open question is how much of it survives full-strength shoves.

Chart 3 · how often it fell (env/fall_rate)

Toddler

When we pushed it more, it fell more. When we pushed it less, it hardly fell.

Child

Each stage starts with gentle bumps and slippery patches, and they get harder over time. The falls rise with them to 1 in 5 runs, which is exactly the level that made the coach cut the goal.

Adult

Falls track the randomisation ramp (0.15 → 1.0 over 2,000 rounds), not the speed. In stage 3 the target sat at walking pace while falls rose to 0.2. This is the direct evidence that the 5 %/15 % thresholds are set below the floor this body reaches under full-strength shoves. Tuned without shoves, they lock the curriculum shut. The final test, run with no shoves, shows 0–1 % falls, so the walk and run are stable when left alone.

What I'd do next

  1. Fix the speed rule (minutes of work, no training). Raise the target only when the athlete is actually within about 0.3 m/s of it, and measure falls against the level the shoves themselves cause (for example, raise under 12 %, back off over 25 %).
  2. Carry on from tonight's final policy instead of starting over (training/checkpoints/run_track/q5_lap_more/latest.pt), with the fixed rule, for another night. Stage 5 was still getting faster when the clock stopped.
  3. Optional now: put tonight's 2.76 m/s runner in the game. It laps about 9 s faster than the shipped one with no falls. But it wasn't trained against other runners bumping it the way the shipped crowd policy was, and it spent its last 11 minutes on gentle shoves. Watch one race before deciding. The files are in training/logs/scratch_policies/; nothing was published.
