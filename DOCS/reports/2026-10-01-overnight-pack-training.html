Overnight speed and pack training

30 September – 1 October 2026 · training 23:12 → 07:51 (8 h 12 min), scoring finished 08:39 · MuJoCo Warp, 8,192 athletes at once, RTX 5070 Ti laptop, shared with a PoOlympic run for most of the night · batch R (training/run_r.ps1)

In one breath: two goals, a faster lap and a runner that survives being bumped. The night's pick does both in part: 3.02 m/s round the lap (33.2 s), up from 2.76 (36.2 s), and 88 % stay on their feet through 20 seconds of being bumped, up from 30 %. A faster runner also came out of the first stage (3.38 m/s, 29.6 s), but only 15 % of those survive a pack, so it was not the pick. Nothing has been put into the game; that is the owner's call, and it has not yet been watched in a real 8-runner race.

The test at the end

Three 20-second runs of 256 athletes each on the rooftop loop, with no random shoves and no added wobble, exactly as the game drives them. Each runner is scored twice: alone, and among two runner-shaped figures that keep re-forming around it (one in front and slower, one coming up behind, one leaning in from the side).

The three charts that matter

Orange is stage 1 (speed), blue is stage 2 (pack). The bottom axis is training rounds; stage 2 begins at round 7,800 because it carries on from there.

Chart 1 · how often it fell (env/fall_rate)

Toddler

Running alone, it almost never fell down. When other runners bumped it, it fell a lot. Then it practised and fell much less.

Child

Alone (orange) there is one wobble right at the start, then about 1 fall in 100 runs all night. With bumping figures (blue) more than half the runs ended in a fall for the first hour. After that it dropped to about 1 in 4, and it was still edging down at the end.

Adult

The orange spike at round 170 is the moment the learner was released after its critic was refitted; it recovered inside 50 rounds, which earlier test runs with the clipped critic never did. The blue plateau near 0.55 for about 1,700 rounds is the pack task at soft-to-full threat with a critic that had to relearn what a bump costs; the drop from round 9,500 is the policy learning to absorb contact. This is a training-time rate with shoves, sensor noise and an exploration spread that grew to 1.3, so it overstates falls: the same final policy scored 12 % down in the deterministic pack test.

Chart 2 · how fast it actually went (env/v_toward)

Toddler

It got faster very quickly, then stopped getting faster. When others got in the way it slowed down.

Child

In the first half hour it went from 2.7 to 3.2 metres a second. The next three hours added almost nothing. In the pack stage it slowed to 2.7, because the figures are literally in its way and it learned to be careful, then crept back up a little.

Adult

Stage 1 is the same ceiling the 15 September runs hit near 3.4 m/s: 1,500 rounds to reach it, 7,500 more for +0.03. That is a stability limit of this gait, not a shortage of training, so more hours of the same recipe will not move it. Stage 2's speed is speed toward the marker while being obstructed, so it is not comparable with the orange line; the scored lap for the final policy is 3.02 m/s alone. The pack stage cost 0.36 m/s of the lap speed stage 1 had found.

Chart 3 · the pace we asked for (env/target_speed)

Toddler

This time we only said "a bit faster" when it was keeping up. It worked. Later we stopped asking, because it was busy not falling over.

Child

Last night the coach asked for 5.5 m/s from a runner doing 1.65, then gave up and asked for walking pace. Tonight the coach stayed just one small step ahead: the goal rose smoothly to 3.4 and stopped when the runner could not close the gap. In the pack stage the goal never moved, because falls stayed above the "safe to speed up" line all the way.

Adult

This is the fix from the previous report, working. The target now rises only when the running fall rate is under 12 % and the athlete is within 0.15 m/s of the current target, in steps of 0.01. It neither ran away nor locked at its floor. In stage 2 the thresholds were 20 % / 40 % and the fall rate never got under 20 %, so the target sat at its 3.01 start: the stage trained robustness at a fixed pace. A longer pack stage should cross 20 % and start raising it.

What had to be fixed to get here

  • The saved training files were gone. This copy of the project had the policy file the game uses but none of the checkpoints. A new tool rebuilds a starting point from the game's policy file; it reproduced the recorded 2.76 m/s lap exactly.
  • Restarting from that wrecked the runner, three times. In six short tests the runner went from no falls to 36–82 % falls within a few hundred rounds of learning being switched back on. With learning frozen it stayed perfect, so the learning itself was the cause. The part that estimates "how well is this going" was only allowed to change very slowly and could not keep up with the runner it was judging. Letting it keep up fixed it.
  • The "raise the speed" rule from the previous report: fixed as described under chart 3.
  • A flaw in my own scoring, caught at 3 a.m. Between stages the script was about to carry the slow starting runner into stage 2, because it multiplied speed by pack survival and the fast runner survives packs badly. Stage 1 is now picked on lap speed alone; the combined score is used only at the end. No training was lost.
  • TensorBoard stopped updating mid-run. Each filmed clip was written as a new file next to the curves, and TensorBoard then ignored the curve file. Clips now go in their own sub-folder.

What this does not show yet

  • The pack figures are not real runners. They are solid, immovable runner-shaped posts on rails. Real athletes give way when hit and have swinging legs to trip over. 88 % in this test is evidence, not proof; the proof is the 8-runner race in the game, where 3 of 8 fell on the phone last night.
  • It has not been run in Unity at all, alone or in a race.
  • Speed is still short of the 15 September best (3.34–3.44 m/s, files lost) once pack training is applied, and the 3.4 wall is untouched.
  • The graphics card was shared with PoOlympic training for about 6 of the 8 hours, so stage 2 got about half the rounds it would have had alone (73,000 steps a second against 145,000 in stage 1).

Suggested next steps

  1. Put tonight's pick in the game as the second lap policy (the slot q5 holds now) and run the 8-runner race in the editor and on the phone. Count falls against last night's 3 of 8.
  2. If it holds up, give the pack stage another night starting from tonight's pick. It was still improving, and once falls drop under 20 % the speed rule will start raising the pace by itself.
  3. Breaking the 3.4 wall needs a change of gait recipe (stride timing, or training with speed from the first step), not more hours.
